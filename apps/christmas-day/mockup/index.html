Christmas Day

A loop of little Christmas stories for a 72 × 16 LED display, from trimming the tree on Christmas Eve to fireworks on Christmas night. A candy-cane clock and the date appear between scenes. The simulator below plays the same frames the bar plays, at the same 15 frames per second, with your current time on the clock card.

    The show

    Eight short scenes follow Christmas from Eve to night. An elf trims the tree and carolers sing under a street lamp. Santa's sleigh crosses the moon. On Christmas morning a cat raids the stockings and a puppy pops out of a present. The toy train gets a stowaway, a snowman loses his hat, and the night ends with fireworks. Every scene has characters that move, a small story and a punchline.

    Between scenes, a clock card shows the bar's local time as a 24-hour HH:MM in a candy-cane pixel font, then the date in gold. It plays over a twinkling light string, two snowy pines and gentle snowfall.

    How it works

    1. Sprites as text

      Every character is a small grid of letters, one per pixel, in Python. Small details such as eyes, coal and noses use lit colors, because a black pixel is an unlit LED.

    2. Render each scene

      A deterministic Python renderer choreographs every frame: sleigh paths, snowfall, fire, fireworks and captions.

    3. Bake into .anim

      Frames go into the bar's own animation format, with a tone curve tuned on real hardware. The firmware plays them smoothly with no network traffic.

    4. Run the show on the bar

      A small TypeScript app loops the scenes and draws the live clock from the bar's time. A test checks it against the renderer, pixel for pixel.

    Key decisions

    • D1Mirror Wizarding Thanksgiving. Pre-rendered full-screen scenes and a live clock card, built on the shared render kit.
    • D2Christmas Eve to Christmas night. The playlist order tells the day as one story.
    • D4The same tone curve as the wizard app. Gamma 0.8 was approved on the real panel.
    • D6No black details. Eyes and coal use lit colors, and backgrounds stay dark but lit.
    • D7A candy-cane clock. White-to-ice time with a red shadow, then a gold date with a holly-green shadow.
    • D9Captions stay clear of the action. Small captions sit in empty sky. In the finale, the village dims under the words.
