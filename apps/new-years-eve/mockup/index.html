Party scenes and a countdown to midnight on a 72 × 16 LED display, then huge digits for the last ten seconds and fireworks with HAPPY NEW YEAR and the year. The simulator below runs the app's own phase logic and draws the same frames and live text the bar does, at 15 frames per second. Jump the clock to watch the countdown and the celebration.

START on the bar does the same as here: it moves the app's clock to 15 seconds before the next new year, and real time comes back after the celebration. OK skips a scene or card. Back quits.

How the night runs

Everything follows the bar's own local clock, so the countdown hits zero exactly when the bar's clock reaches midnight. The year is read from the bar's date.

  1. Dec 31 · until T−60 s

    Party

    Eight scenes, each followed by a countdown card with the time left and the year. A scene only starts if it finishes before the final minute.

  2. T−60 … T−11 s

    Final minute

    The seconds count down over chasing lights while a bar fills. The lights speed up for the last 30 seconds.

  3. T−10 … T−1 s

    Final ten

    One huge digit per second on the bar's tick, each in a new color, with a flash on every beat.

  4. 00:00:00 · 60 s

    Midnight

    A huge 0, a burst, then HAPPY NEW YEAR and the year over a fireworks loop.

  5. Jan 1 · until 06:00

    After midnight

    The party carries on with a clock card that says HAPPY and the new year.

  6. Any other day

    Off-season

    The same party loop, with a clock card counting the days to the new year.

Live screens

Everything that depends on the time is drawn live by the app over a looping backdrop: the countdown card, the final minute, the final ten and the celebration. Click a frame to jump the bar's clock to that moment.

How it's built

A Python renderer draws every scene and backdrop as pixel art and bakes them into the bar's .anim format, which the firmware plays smoothly with no network traffic. A small TypeScript app on the bar picks what to show from its local time and draws the live text as pixel-font bitmaps, one draw per second at most.

The phase logic, the countdown text and the screen layouts are plain functions shared by the bar app, its unit tests and this page. A parity test checks the app's live text against the renderer, pixel for pixel.
