Lunar New Year

Festival scenes for a 72 × 16 LED display: lanterns, a dragon dance, a lion dance, red envelopes, dumplings, firecrackers and fireworks, plus the zodiac animal of the year. A clock card and a countdown card appear between scenes. The simulator below plays the same frames the bar plays, at 15 frames per second, with your current time.

    The show

    Seven scenes celebrate the festival. Sky lanterns rise over curled rooftops. A long dragon on poles chases a pearl down the street. A lion dances to a drum and plucks the greens. A child bows to a grandparent and gets a red envelope. Dumplings steam, and one hides a lucky coin. Firecrackers scare off the Nian. The night ends with red-and-gold fireworks.

    A short zodiac scene shows the animal of the current or upcoming lunar year: twelve versions, picked from the bar's date. Before the new year, the count card shows N DAYS / TO LUNAR NEW YEAR. From New Year's Day through the Lantern Festival (day 15), it shows YEAR OF THE / GOAT, HAPPY / NEW YEAR (on day 15, LANTERN / FESTIVAL) and GONG XI / FA CAI. The clock card shows the time and then the date.

    Holiday mode: START on the bar (or the button above) previews the festival on any date. Each further press steps to the next of the twelve animals. After all twelve, it returns to the real date.

    How it works

    1. Sprites as text

      The zodiac animals, the dragon's head and the lion are small grids of letters in Python. Eyes and other small details use lit colors, because a black pixel is an unlit LED.

    2. Render each scene

      A deterministic Python renderer choreographs every frame: the dragon's wave, the lion's leap, every firecracker's pop and the fireworks.

    3. Bake into .anim

      Frames go into the bar's own animation format, with a tone curve tuned on real hardware. The firmware plays them smoothly with no network traffic.

    4. Follow the calendar

      A small TypeScript app reads the bar's date, picks the playlist and the zodiac animal, and draws the live cards. Tests check it against real dates and against the renderer, pixel for pixel.

    Key decisions

    • D2Seven festival scenes. Lanterns, two dances, family, food, firecrackers, fireworks.
    • D5Twelve short zodiac scenes. Six seconds each, so all twelve stay small.
    • D6Two playlists. The festival opens with the year's animal and its greeting.
    • D10START walks the zodiac. Thirteen presses review all twelve animals and return.
    • D11Beyond the date table. A generic greeting and no countdown.
    • D12Respectful figures. Simple shapes, no faces, no caricature.
